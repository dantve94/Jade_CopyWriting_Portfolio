### The importance of seeking professional advice for frenulum issues

If you find yourself with a suspicion that you may have a frenulum issue we highly recommend seeking professional help. To start you off, we have written a comprehensive guide to help you to understand some of the frenulum issues that might concern men as well as go through some of the most pertinent concerns. Some common treatments for frenulum issues will also be discussed and, most importantly, we will go over why it is absolutely necessary to consult your healthcare professional when it comes to any frenulum issues you may be experiencing. Included are also anonymous cases provided by the New York Urology Specialists.

#### Understanding frenulum issues

If the tissue connecting the prepuce (foreskin) and glans (the head of the penis), is causing pain or discomfort, it is likely that there is a frenulum issue. There are a few signs that point toward frenulum issues:

- **The penis position:** The penis will point downwards due to the penile frenulum being restrictive.
- **Pain:** When engaging in sexual activities or intercourse the frenulum becomes painful during arousal.
- **Bleeding:** The frenulum bleeds or tears during sexual intercourse or other strenuous activities.
- **Difficulty when retracting the foreskin:** If you struggle to retract your foreskin, it is possible that your frenulum is short or tight.

#### Top frenulum issues and concerns

While there exist an array of potential challenges when it comes to the penis and more specifically the frenulum, studies have narrowed down some of the top penile and penile frenulum concerns faced by men. These include:

- **Frenulum Breve:** Frenulum Breve is a penile disorder. When the skin on the underside of the penis attaching the foreskin to the penis is too tight. This usually happens because the frenulum is short. This could make it challenging to keep the area clean as well as cause pain during erections and intercourse.
- **Frenular chordee:** When an individual has Frenular chordee, their penis bends downwards. This is as a result, again, of a short frenulum.
- **Hypospadias:** When Hypospadias occurs, the urethra isn't in its usual place. Oftentimes the opening of the urethra is on the underside of the penis.
- **Premature ejaculation:** There is a lot of research that is being done around the relationship between frenulum issues and premature ejaculation. According to the National Institute of Health (NIH), studies have shown that up to 43% of individuals affected by premature ejaculation reported a short frenulum or other frenulum-related issues. This is not always the case, however.
- **Scarring:** Scarring also affects the penile frenulum. If any tears occur (even micro ones) there is a possibility that, on healing, scar tissue will develop.
- **Dyspareunia:** This simply means painful intercourse. In a lot of the cases we see regarding frenulum issues, individuals report pain during sexual intercourse.

#### What are some common treatments involving the frenulum?

There are treatments available for frenulum issues that range from minimally invasive and topical to more procedure-based solutions. It is always best to contact a professional to assist and advise you with any frenulum and related issues. Treatment options include, but are not limited to:

- **Stretching exercises:** Your doctor might suggest simple stretches you can do to alleviate tension. However, the frenulum tissue is not as responsive to stretching as other tissue in the body.
- **Steroid creams or lotions:** In some instances, you may be prescribed products containing steroids.
- **Frenuloplasty:** Frenuloplasty involves the lengthening of the frenulum surgically.
- **Frenulectomy:** This is where the whole frenulum is removed.
- **Circumcision:** This surgery removes the foreskin of the penis.

#### Why is a professional consultation necessary?

Professional consultation is absolutely necessary when you are ready to take the first step to treating any frenulum-related issue because there are risks involved in some treatments like frenuloplasty and a frenulectomy. Some of these risks include:

- **Pain and discomfort:** Although it is normal to have some pain and discomfort, there is the risk of it becoming more severe and even unmanageable.
- **Swelling and bruising:** There will likely be some swelling and bruising but excessive swelling and bruising might need to be addressed.
- **Infection:** If you have any suspicion that you might be developing an infection, you will need treatment.
- **Scarring:** It is possible to develop scar tissue which may require further surgery.
- **Haematoma:** This is where blood collects under the skin.
