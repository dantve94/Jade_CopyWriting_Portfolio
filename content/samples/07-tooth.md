### Tooth

I felt like pancakes when I woke up this morning. I imagined the sweet scent of butter melting in the pan as I lay in bed. It's been rough, this life thing lately. I find myself floating, pleasure-seeking and chasing. Chasing hits of dopamine wherever and however I can get them. Was it my stress causing me to seek little moments of respite? Perhaps it is the big sad knocking at my front door...again.

While in the past I have tried to welcome him as a friend. Before I have treated him gently trying to release my limbs from his sticky grasp but lately I resent him. He robs me. He drains the colour from my days and I find myself many mornings dried out and too exhausted to function. But this morning I felt like pancakes.

So, for the first time in a few days, or was it many days?, I got out of bed with the shadow of a purpose. A pancake purpose. I put my feet on the floor and moved the blanket to the side of the bed freeing myself from its weight. A weight I had welcomed for a while. Something to ground my existential floating. I got up. I actually did it. I got up.

I stumbled into the kitchen. I was making the coffee for us this morning. My loving husband slowly rising from his sleepy night. I love making him coffee. I love waking him up gently with an act of love. I have been the one receiving lately. Receiving and possibly even not reciprocating even though I know, on the days I am okay, my heart burns for him. It's just this limbo that threatens to suck me in.

But not this morning! I didn't get back into bed this morning for my extra hour of lucid sleep. I opened the fridge. I opened it with a goal, my first ounce of determination for a while now. Did I have milk? No. Was I going to let it hold me back? Not this morning. The pancakes were too hopeful and surely within my reach.

So I made a plan. I walked to my mom's house a block away and let myself in. She hasn't been home for a while. I know she needs space too. Space to say goodbye to an absent father who is facing his mortality and failing kidneys. Years of abusing alcohol finally bearing its consequences. That aside, she wasn't home.

I opened her cupboards...bare. If I could fill them I would. You can't eat love unfortunately. I was lucky enough to find an egg and just enough milk for the consistency of good pancake batter. I made a mental note to replace it all as soon as I could. My walk home was mindful. I was aware of the threatening sadness but also thinking about the sugar and cinnamon that awaited me. Cinnamon tastes like hope, you know.

Back in my cold, dark kitchen I opened a blind. Dust slowly falling and catching the sunlight in such a way that might actually be appreciated. In a way that held a beauty, a gentleness as it caught the new day's soft light in its presence. I looked out into the field. I don't stand here often enough. Well, I do but lately I haven't been seeing. Looking maybe, but not seeing.

It was pancake time. I scurried around the kitchen looking for pans, bowls and mixers. It was on. I had just the right amount of everything and the serendipity was a warm shower on an oh-so-cold body, stiff from staying in one place for too long. I measured, I mixed and I spooned and my dreamy morning vision came to life as the smell of butter filled the kitchen. A "good morning" to my family. A message that I was here, that I wasn't too far gone, that I was fighting.

The day enveloped me in gratitude because I showed up for it. I took off my jersey and embraced the sun outside. It was way too bright for my eyes, sad and dark for so long, but I knew it was time to see the light...literally. With pancakes a-go, I felt hope rising. A sense of relief, I made it. A sense of purpose, I will be okay and a sense of gratitude, I am through another dark tunnel and into the light. With a grateful, light heart I ate my pancakes with my family. I bit into the soft, sweet pancake and heard a crunch. I broke my tooth.
