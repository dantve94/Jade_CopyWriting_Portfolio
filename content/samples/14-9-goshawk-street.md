### 9 Goshawk Street

Maya couldn't believe it. Driving into the new town, she knew she was screwed. It was so quiet, even run down you might say, and now she had to live here? Damn her parents for deciding to move to the outskirts of the West Coast. The houses were white but they had a film of dirt, all of them. As they drove into the street that was now her street, she noticed there were faded words on the wall of her new house. "Goshawk St" it said. Number 7, too. What luck?

She hated it so much. She wanted to go home to her old school and her friends. At least she had Rascal, her maltese puppy. He was growing though. Where were the people, anyway? she asked herself as her parents pulled into the driveway. She opened the car door and Rascal whimpered. He didn't want to get out of the car either.

After the longest, most restless few nights in the new house she woke up with a bit of energy again. Her mom was in the kitchen baking bran muffins. She was trying too hard to make Maya feel at home. Maya knew it wasn't her mom's fault that her dad had to move them here to work on the new oil rig. Still, she felt uneasy and couldn't quite say why.

Maya was determined to make this work. She decided to take Rascal for a walk. He needed to get out of the house too. She put his harness on and rummaged through a few boxes before she found his leash. Finally, Rascal was ready for his walk. She opened the door and he sat down. He wouldn't budge.

She picked him up. She knew he needed this, or was it her that needed this? Walking down the street, a potent smell caught her off guard. Rascal barked. What was that smell? She looked at the neighbour's house. Was it coming from there? Something about the house made her feel edgy. Like it didn't belong in this town. She couldn't quite say why. As she passed it, Rascal pulled on the leash yanking her to the other side of the street. He kept pulling until he backed out of his collar and ran down the road.

Maya panicked. Rascal had disappeared. She saw him run through the bushes on the side of the road and she ran toward them. She called Rascal but the world just became quieter and quieter. The wind stopped blowing, the whole town fell silent and Rascal was gone.

With a growing fear inside, she felt like throwing up. She had one responsibility, keep Rascal safe and she didn't. He was out there now, and her instincts came up with no ideas at all. She did the only thing she could. She began searching.

On her way back home with no luck a glimmer of hope showered down her body. She saw a child outside the neighbour's house. A young girl with a solemn face. They made eye contact and the girl dashed inside the house. What was it about this house? It wasn't just the smell. It was the only house in the whole town with green grass. The child ran inside. She was determined to find Rascal though and, although her whole body was screaming no, she knocked on the neighbour's door. Just once.

A sweet man answered. She shared her plight and he invited her to look inside the house for Rascal. She thought it unlikely but she didn't want to be rude and she was desperate. That strange smell was back. It wasn't coming from outside in, it was inside leaking out.

It started to get darker and darker as she went in, until she could barely see. Suddenly stairs appeared. "Come along, dear. Not much further to go" the old man said. She didn't want to be impolite so she kept following him, keeping her eyes peeled for Rascal until she realised where she was. The old man had led her into a bunker in the basement. He slammed the door behind her and all her hope drained from her body through her feet.

She smelled that putrid smell mixed with the smell of Earth and she felt a stinging sensation as the soil from above dropped onto her skin. The grass roots were long and with a cold realisation and burning skin, she realised the grass above her was carnivorous and she was its fertiliser.

In her final moments, as the roots closed around her throat, Maya realised: the ground above them wasn’t just grass—it was feeding, consuming everything beneath it. The town had been abandoned for a reason, and she now understood why the grass at 9 Goshawk was always greener.

Her final breath was swallowed by the earth.
