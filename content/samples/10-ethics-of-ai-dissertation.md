### The ethics of Artificial Intelligence and the South African consumers’ right to privacy viewed through a philosophical lens

<p class="excerpt-note">Bachelor of Arts Honours in Strategic Brand Communication, The IIE. An excerpt: the abstract and the research framing from Chapter 1.</p>

#### Abstract

Artificial Intelligence (AI) and soft biometrics are becoming increasingly apparent in everyday life. This is especially true in marketing. It is important to evaluate their use and ethical implications. The purpose of this research is to explore the use of Artificial Intelligence by businesses in South Africa and subsequent ethical practices with a specific focus on consumer privacy. Using a cross-sectional analysis this study shows that AI is used to gather private information and not all businesses have acted ethically with regards to consumer privacy. In terms of ethical philosophy 80% of the philosophical theories consulted found this to be unethical. Further studies are needed to uncover more information regarding consumer privacy.

#### Rationale

More people are going online than ever before because of COVID 19. The whole demographic of online shoppers is changing drastically. A lot of these people are not used to being online and it is important to know if their privacy will be treated ethically. One of the suggested risks of introducing this kind of AI, and others, into the lives of South Africans, is a loss of autonomy and privacy. It is important to question how artificial intelligence is used and regulated. Artificial intelligence is already prevalent in our world in helpful and life-saving ways but it still needs to be evaluated.

Artificial intelligence is penetrating our environment through channels like manufacturing, retailing, banking and health care. While we are pushing the boundaries of our technological capabilities, it is important to question how artificial intelligence is used especially when it comes to affecting humans, like their right to privacy.

#### Problem statement

Marketers could potentially be using AI unethically to elicit private information from consumers in South Africa through the soft biometrics on their devices and by using artificial intelligence in situations excluding opt-in marketing. This is a problem because in some cases consumers have not explicitly consented to the use of their information in such a manner and sometimes they don’t know what they are opting into. This research sets out to determine whether artificial intelligence is ethical when it is used to better marketing tactics via personal information, and curating adverts for the consumers they are targeted towards.

#### Research question

Is the use of soft biometrics and artificial intelligence ethical when it comes to South African consumers’ right to privacy?

#### Research objectives

I. To explore the use of AI by businesses in terms of privacy.

II. To assess the ethical practices of businesses in South Africa in terms of privacy.
