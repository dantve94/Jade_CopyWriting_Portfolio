### The House That Remembered

I loved my childhood home. With its creaky floors and oddly painted walls. My mother couldn't decide on a colour when it came time to paint the house, so we settled on them all. I remember the Sundays we played board games and the nights listening to the floors creak as my parents danced their nights away. I remember many sunny days and many bright rooms. Although we were never allowed in gran's room I am sure it was sunny too. She moved away when I was young. I can't quite remember why.

My mother would always hum the Für Elise. I heard it coming from my gran's room once too, though my gran never spoke. My mom must have been in there at the time. I only ever tried to go into my gran's room once, it was locked. Come to think of it, it's a bit strange. I could laugh about it now. I didn't question it at the time.

Then one day my gran disappeared. I smelled her perfume everywhere for months still, but she never came back. My mom told us she "moved away". Nobody ever mentioned her again, normal right? I guess in the back of my mind there were things unseen, things unsaid. Soft voices behind the locked door, urgent but muffled, like the house itself was swallowing its words. In the morning, everything was as it should be. Breakfast on the table. Smiling faces.

Childhood imagination. Nothing more. Right? I had not returned to the house in decades. But in my mind, I wandered its halls freely, reliving those golden days. Until—

A storm. A night where something was wrong.

Had I heard crying? Raised voices? Or worse—a silence so dense it pressed against my ears? That couldn't be right. My childhood was happy. The scars on my body were from playing outside like normal children do. Right?

The way certain memories seemed blurred, as though someone had pressed a careful hand against my past. I shook my head. No. The house had been warm. Full of love. I was sure of it. We had moved suddenly. No warning. I remembered packing in a single afternoon, my mother's hands trembling as she folded my clothes into a suitcase.

Had I seen something?

A flash of something beyond that locked door? A figure in the shadows? A secret I was never meant to know? A key detail surfaced. The last time I passed the locked door, it had been open—just a crack. A sliver of darkness. A pair of grey eyes staring back at me. Had that been real?

I didn't know. And maybe I never would.

I sighed, pushing away the strange unease that coiled in my chest. Some memories faded with time, that was all. The house was golden in the summer. The light through the lace curtains. The comforting tick of the clock. I would remember it that way.

Even if the house remembered otherwise.
